## 4. Abstract

The abstract is going to consist of 5 short sections (reminder: 150 words maximum)

1. Context - why online age assurance is being introduced
2. Purpose - what my article is actually trying to examine; the ethical question
3. Approach - literature-based analysis using academic and official sources
4. Main findings - the main pros and cons found
5. Conclusion - my overall judgement

Note-to-self: Do not use the abstract to introduce points that do not appear in the main article.

## 5. Main article

### 5.1. Introduction

Aiming to include around 100-130 words

Ideas on what to include:
- Introduce growing use of age checks on online platforms
- Briefly explain age assurance and distinguish it from full identity verification
- State ethical issue - child protection versus privacy and anonymity
- Briefly identify the sections that follow.

### 5.2. Why online age assurance is being introduced

Around 120-150 words, covering:
- online risks affecting children;
- the purpose of age assurance;
- the UK regulatory context;
- the difference between estimating age and verifying identity;
- why platforms and governments are interested in these systems.

This section should establish the problem only, no arguments to be made.

### 5.3. Privacy and cybersecurity risks

180-220 words. Most likely the largest portion of the article.

Possible subtopics:
- collection of identity or biometric data;
- data breaches and identity theft;
- tracking and loss of online anonymity;
- inaccurate age estimation;
- risks of centralised databases;
- data retention and secondary uses;
- risks faced by marginalised or vulnerable users;
- compliance with data-protection principles.

Not every risk needs to be assessed. I'll select 2-3 to discuss properly.

### 5.4. Ethical evaluation: can the risks be justified?

Ideally wanting around 150-180 words here.

Here, I'll use ethical principles to evaluate the evidence. Examples:
- Is more personal data collected than necessary? (Privacy)
- Can users access the internet without excessive monitoring? (Autonomy)
- Are the measures appropriate to the risks being addressed? (Proportionality)
- Do the systems work equally well for different users? (Fairness)
- Does the method introduce risks greater than the problem it addresses? (Security)

I could also compare privacy-invasive approaches with privacy-preserving approaches. For example, proving that someone is over a certain age without revealing their full identity.

### 5.5. Conclusion

100-120 words to end it off.

To be included are the following:
- a reminder of the article’s purpose;
- my direct answer to the research question;
- the most important supporting reasons from my point of view;
- a final condition/recommendation (for example, that age assurance should be proportionate, secure, accurate, privacy-preserving, etcetera).

No new arguments are to be introduced here.
